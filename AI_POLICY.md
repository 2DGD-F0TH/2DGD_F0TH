On the usage of Large Language Models
=====================================

TL;DR
-----

In the following, the terms "AI", "LLM", "GPT", "Artificial Intelligence" will all be conflated under the term "Large Language Models".

- Any contribution must state if it was generated by, or with the assistance of, Large Language Models. Such contributions will be kindly refused, but appreciation will be shown for taking the interest and time to contribute.
- Any contribution that is found to be generated by (or with the assistance of) Large Language Models and passed as human-generated content will be immediately removed. Such contribution will be assumed to be done in bad faith and the contributor will be immediately banned from further contributions.
- LLM ingestion of this work is forbidden, no matter the reason (be it training, summarizing, trying to understand the content or any other kind of reason).

The longer version
------------------

The last 2 years have seen a huge spike in growth of "AI" or "Artificial Intelligence", in the form of "Generative AI".

As with many other technologies that have seen what I perceive to be an "unnatural push" from the industry, I find myself trying to understand the "why"s of such push and if whether such technologies are bringing some advantages that I'm unaware of.

What I have observed is concerning.

### Ethical concerns

It seems the main objective is to lead people away from the source materials, keeping users in certain ecosystems.

In the field of "text generation", users find their answer directly given by their search engine of choice, damaging the owners of the source materials who might be using advertisements to fund their own endeavour, or check their analytics to see if their work is appreciated.

In the field of "image, audio and video generation" things get even scarier. It takes a few seconds of speech to clone someone's voice to a believable degree, drawings and paintings are generated on a whim and realistic videos are a few clicks away. This damages artists, audio engineers, film makers but also opens the door to something much more sinister: believable fakes.

Thanks to "AI", we have entered what is shaping to be a "post-truth era".

The law has not caught up yet: there is currently no way for a project who wants to be at least a bit "open to people's contributions" to defend itself against the wild and unfettered scraping done by bots that feeds models for Generic Pre-Trained Transformers (GPT).

So the only thing I can do is saying: **Do not use AI on this project.**

If you want to contribute using AI, please don't.

If you want to summarize this project by having an LLM ingest it, this isn't the right project for you. This book is meant to be in-depth and detailed, the mere act of summarizing it would defeat its purpose and having a stochastic text generator do it for you is even more inane.

### Copyright concerns

A special mention is deserved to copyright, something almost everyone loves to hate.

But being "at the other side" of the copyright law, as a creator (among all the other contributors who have willingly given time and knowledge to this project), it pains me immensely to see something else take what has been created here and flip it to an unsuspecting user without even giving the proper attribution.

Any contribution made with AI has the potential to contain copyrighted material, without the knowledge of me or whoever is contributing.

Any ingestion and "flipping" could be considered (from a "common sense" standpoint) as a derivative work, then any LLM that doesn't credit this body of knowledge would be infringing (the spirit of) the "attribution" part of the Creative Commons BY-NC-SA (Attribution - Non-Commercial - Share Alike).

The copyright situation of any AI-generated work is messy at best, and I don't want to deal with this kind of headaches.

### Environmental Concerns

LLMs require a whole lot of energy, both to run and to be trained. If you never did, look online how to run a small AI model locally and see how slow it is and how much it revs up your CPU (or GPU, if you have a fairly modern one).

If you want to have a somewhat believable experience with a local model, 7 billion parameters is the minimum you need, which means at least 8GB of (V)RAM and a very powerful CPU (or GPU).

Computing units consume a lot of energy doing statistical analysis, natural language processing and understanding, tokenization and generating a response. This has an impact on pollution.

### Health Concerns

Some smaller studies (including at least one from MIT) seem to suggest that users of LLMs have lower brain activity and engagement when undertaking SAT-level assessments. This may suggest that abusing LLMs may harm our ability to think critically, which is something that I personally believe (and have grown to appreciate more and more in these few years) is absolutely fundamental.

I personally prefer a wrong, but natural, critical thought than something regurgitated by a statistical melting pot.

### Societal Concerns

As stated before, we have entered a "post-truth era": it is easy to make someone say or do something they've never done. This can destroy lives.

Some people are making deepfakes, others are in love with LLMs because "they are empathetic" (they are not, they're giving a realistic looking response to their input), others even have AI-partners or AI-psychologists.

Society is moving towards more isolation, people are going to be more lonely and less willing to engage with others. Why?

Because people are complex and engaging with them is difficult.

It is easier to have a "yes-machine" that responds in the right way to stimulate some dopamine out of your brain.

Breaking down communities and dealing with "on-demand dopamine dispensers" will make reality hit harder: people will become less willing to be patient, they will be ruder if they have to wait or the will look at you weird if you dare to say "I don't know" to a question.

Wherever a new data center is created, the promise of prosperity is brought. What is instead given to the populace is higher energy bills, because energy companies need to monetize and eventually even restructure the power lines to support such huge power draw.

As already stated above, I feel like the AI providers are trying to enact strategies to make themselves "irreplaceable" with the final objective of having their technology become so engrained in our culture that people will panic the moment an AI service goes down for 10 minutes.

AI services are currently sold at a loss, which further reinforces (in my mind) such theory.

AI has also changed the contract of the Internet itself: before the wild botting was happening, you would search for something, find it and move on. Now you will find more and more sites that implement "proof of work" measures to limit the scraping by AI-related bots: this is because traffic doesn't come for free, and scraping is costing people hundreds if not thousands.

### Quality Concerns

Getting down to brass tacks, this is essentially a copy-paste from the old stance on AI.

AI is an ethical mess right now: books, blogs, microblogging posts are all melded together without any regard for copyright, correctness or quality into a result that is "semantically and syntactically *probably* correct" but is of inferior quality to the sum of its sources.

To that we have to add the issue of AI self-feeding: AI-generated solutions and posts are invading the Internet, which in turn will enter the generative AI training loop, lowering the quality of responses further.

This sees the addition of willful poisoning by people who are against the widespread diffusion of "AI". Systems are created that will let the AI scan an infinite number of pages containing completely meaningless content.

### Countering some excuses

#### "The cost per token is getting lower"

Yes, that is true.

Let me bring in diets: if a certain food suddenly gets half the calories, but you eat three times as much, you shouldn't ask yourself why you're getting fatter.

This is what's happening with AI: it is an arm's race. Each model's life cycle lasts less than the previous one (so re-training happens more often), each model ingests and generates more tokens. If the costs per token is halved, but in one year you find that the industry is producing or ingesting 10 times as many tokens, the total energy cost if 5x.

That is not getting lower.

And the industry itself is based on cutthroat competition. The energy cost won't get lower. The "cost per token" is (in my most humble opinion) a short-sighted metric.

#### "AI is just a tool"

Yes, it is.

I would make an equivalence to heavy machinery: it is cool to see, but it will hurt you and everyone around you a lot the moment you forget what it is and to use it with due care.

It is your choice how to use a tool, but it is also my own choice to decide that I don't want to clean the mess that such tool has created.

My stance on AI
---------------

Even though AI can be an undeniably useful tool in some situations, I seriously don't like the thought of seeing my (and other people's) hard work being mixed into what is essentially a probabilistic melting pot that can be used by unprincipled people for their own personal gain.

This means that any contribution that is found to be the product of generative AI will also immediately be deleted and the contributor may be banned from making further contributions if the usage of AI was done in bad faith.

This also means that any use of this book to train generative AI is **forbidden**.

AI is an ethical mess right now: books, blogs, microblogging posts are all melded together without any regard for copyright, correctness or quality into a result that is "semantically and syntactically *probably* correct" but is of inferior quality to the sum of its sources.

To that we have to add the issue of AI self-feeding: AI-generated solutions and posts are invading the Internet, which in turn will enter the generative AI training loop, lowering the quality of responses further.

I know that this is a further restriction on the "free as in freedom" principle of this project, and this book may not be "the ultimate Body of Knowledge" on game development, but I want it to be at least somewhat curated.
